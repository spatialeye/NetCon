# Graph Theory - History and Context

Graphs provide a compact way to describe connectivity and have become the standard language for modeling networks. This page gives a short background on topology and graph theory, followed by a brief historical context.

## Topology and network topology

Topology (from Greek "topos", place, and "logos", study) is the branch of mathematics concerned with properties of space that are preserved under continuous deformation (objects may be stretched but not torn or glued). It grew out of geometry, but unlike geometry it does not focus on metric properties such as distance. Instead, it focuses on how a space is structured, such as connectedness and orientation. See [Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology).

Network topology is the topological structure of a network and can be described as:
- **Physical topology:** the real-world connectivity of assets.
- **Logical topology:** a schematic or operational view used for reasoning and analysis.

Network topology is an application of graph theory. See [Network topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_topology).

## Graph theory basics

Graph theory studies the properties of graphs. A graph is a set of points (vertices or nodes) with some of those points connected by lines (edges or links). Depending on the use case, edges can be directed (arrows), forming a directed graph (digraph). Edges can also carry weights such as distance, cost, or capacity, forming a weighted graph. See [Graph theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_theory).

## Historical context

- 1736: [Leonhard Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler) solves the [Seven Bridges of Konigsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Bridges_of_Konigsberg), widely regarded as the start of graph theory.
- 1847: [Gustav Kirchhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Kirchhoff) applies graphs to analyze electrical circuits, linking graph theory to network analysis.
- 1857: [Arthur Cayley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Cayley) studies trees, an important graph class later used in chemistry and network design.
- 1959: [Edsger W. Dijkstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra) publishes the shortest path algorithm, foundational for routing and tracing.
- Today: graphs are central to GIS connectivity, utility networks, transport, social networks, and knowledge graphs.
